How it works

The target is the half-hourly Market Index price published by Elexon, an average of short-term trades in each half-hour. Every morning at 08:00 UTC, before the day-ahead auctions, the forecast for the next UK day is made from what had been published by then.

The inputs are the National Demand Forecast, the wind generation forecast and the forecast daily peak demand, each the latest version published before the cutoff; residual demand (demand minus wind) and wind's share of demand; the time of day, day of the week and month; and recent prices. Elexon publishes prices in batches, sometimes more than two hours late, so a price only counts as known 6 hours after its half-hour ends.

The model is gradient-boosted decision trees (scikit-learn), trained to minimise the average absolute error because prices sometimes spike. It is refitted at the start of every month on all earlier data. The live forecast on the front page is made by the same code that produced the backtest.

The simplest baseline copies the price from the same half-hour two days earlier, because at 08:00 most of the previous day's prices have not happened yet. Using yesterday's price would be look-ahead. A test in the code scrambles everything published after a date and fails if any earlier forecast changes.

In winter the half-hourly demand forecast for most of the next day comes out at 08:45 UTC, after the cutoff, so for November to March the model mostly relies on the daily peak forecast instead. The cutoff was not moved to get round this.

Limitations

There is no gas or carbon price yet. Gas plants usually set the GB price, so this is the most likely improvement. Solar is not modelled separately; it partly shows up in the demand forecast. The model gives one number per half-hour, with no range.
